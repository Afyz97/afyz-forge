# Research methods

| Method | Answers | Participants | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| User interviews | Underlying needs and motivations | 5-8 | 2-4 weeks |
| Usability testing | Whether a specific design or flow works | 5-8 | 1-2 weeks |
| Surveys | How widespread an attitude or preference is | 100+ | 1-2 weeks |
| Card sorting | How people group and label content (information architecture) | 15-30 | 1 week |
| Diary studies | Behaviour as it unfolds over time | 10-15 | 2-8 weeks |
| A/B testing | Which of two specific design options performs better | Until statistically significant | 1-4 weeks |

Choosing:
- Unknown problem space -> interviews. Known design, unknown usability -> usability test.
- "How many people..." -> survey. Navigation or naming -> card sort. Habits over weeks -> diary.
- A/B tests compare options you already have; they do not tell you why.
- Five-user usability rounds surface most major issues; run another round after each fix.
