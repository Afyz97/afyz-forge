# Public Websites & Landing Pages

Brand-mode surfaces: seen once, on the way in, judged in seconds. The job is **comprehension +
emotion + conversion** with a distinctive identity — without sliding into generic AI-SaaS template
output.

## Escape the template

The default AI landing page — centered hero, purple→blue gradient, three feature cards with tinted
icon squares, fake logos, identical section rhythm — reads as "generated." Avoid it by making
**intentional, product-true choices**:

- **One distinctive move.** Pick a single memorable idea (a type treatment, a layout stance, a color
  logic, a signature visual) and commit. Everything else stays disciplined so the one move lands.
- **Real identity, not a fashionable skin.** Derive color, type, and voice from the *actual* brand
  and audience — a security company and a kids' app should not share a look.
- **Vary section rhythm by purpose.** Break the "heading + 3 cards" loop; alternate layout, density,
  and alignment so the page has a scan rhythm.

## Structure that converts (AIDA-ish, not rigid)

- **Hero** — one clear value proposition in the user's words (not "next-gen solutions"), one primary
  CTA, and a supporting visual that shows the actual product/outcome. Keep it clean, spacious, and
  legible on a small laptop — don't bury the message under effects.
- **Proof & specifics** — what it does, concretely; real outcomes; honest social proof. **Never
  fabricate** metrics, testimonials, or "as seen in" logos — use real ones or omit (mark placeholders
  clearly). See AD1-AD10 (`../../../references/shared/ai-defaults.md`).
- **Progressive depth** — skimmable first, detailed as you scroll; a scanner and a reader both served.
- **Clear next step** — a single, repeated primary action; secondary paths (docs, pricing) subordinate.
- **Footer** — real navigation, legal, trust; not an afterthought.
- **Collections** (galleries, awards, archives, showcases) — pick filters, year selectors, featured
  items and detail views by collection size and how people explore it. Bespoke category marks,
  seals or certificates only where they carry meaning and stay scalable. An event window's
  countdown and milestone timeline: the `ui-countdown` skill.

## Craft that reads as premium

- **Typography does the heavy lifting** — a confident type system carries more perceived quality than
  any effect. See `typography-structure-motion.md`.
- **Space and restraint** — luxury and trust come from what you leave out. See `../../ui-styles/references/styles/minimalism.md`,
  the luxury profile in `../../ui-styles/references/style-router.md`.
- **Motion with restraint** — a little purposeful motion (reveal on scroll, a hero moment) adds
  polish; everything animating adds noise. Respect reduced motion. See M1 (`../../../references/shared/visual-system.md`).
- **Imagery is art-directed**, relevant, and performance-budgeted (the effects budget (`../../../references/shared/visual-system.md`)).

## Non-negotiables

- **Mobile is the majority** — design mobile as its own context, not a collapsed desktop.
- **Performance is UX** — LCP on the hero, no layout shift, budgeted media/fonts (the effects budget (`../../../references/shared/visual-system.md`)).
- **SEO-aware semantic structure** — one `<h1>`, meaningful heading order, landmarks, real link text,
  descriptive `alt`, metadata; semantics aid both SEO and accessibility (the user-first-ux accessibility floor (`ui-accessibility` skill)).
- **Accessible** — contrast, focus, keyboard, targets; a beautiful inaccessible page still fails.

## Common AI failure modes

Generic centered gradient hero · "empower your workflow" copy · three-card feature grid with icon
squares · fake logos/testimonials/metrics · identical section rhythm · everything centered · hero
buried under effects · desktop stacked for mobile · unbudgeted media/fonts · missing semantic
structure.
